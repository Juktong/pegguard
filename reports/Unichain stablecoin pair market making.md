# Unichain stable pools reward code, not capital

As of 13 October 2026, stablecoin-pair market making on Unichain makes money on each dollar traded, but the market is too small to justify committing capital. All stable/stable venues on the chain together hold about **$0.3M of liquidity**. They traded about **$5.3–5.4M in the last 30 days** and paid LPs only about **$373–400 in fees**, and no incentive campaign is live anywhere on the chain. One pool carries the market: Uniswap v4 USDC/USDT0 at 0.01% with no hook, holding **$149–160k TVL** on **$1.12–1.36M of 30-day volume**. Its LPs net about **+0.65 bp per dollar traded** after 0.35 bp of adverse selection, and with one-second blocks and $0.0007 swaps, LVR and gas are negligible. Flow is the binding constraint. A $100k book netting 0.65 bp would need roughly **four times the pool's entire volume** to clear a ~3.7–4% risk-free-ish hurdle (Fed funds 3.75–4.00%, sUSDS 3.80%). Only a ~$10k tight-range LP clears that bar, and it earns tens of dollars a month. The risk is in the tails, not in day-to-day price diffusion. Pyth's stablecoin feeds on Unichain have not been updated in 406 days, Pyth's Hermes price service now needs an API key with paid plans after a free trial, Chainlink has no USDT feed on the chain, and USDT0 adds LayerZero bridge and contract-upgrade risk on top of issuer risk. For PegGuard, the most important finding is in its own code. Read line by line, the GUARD breaker trips on the de-trended gap between pool and oracle, not on how far the oracle has moved from the peg. Once tripped, it charges its guard fee on the side that does not drain LPs in an oracle-led depeg. And any oracle failure bypasses it, including a confidence spike. These are code-reading findings; no depeg-scenario test confirms them yet. The response is to rebuild GUARD as a breaker driven by a ratio of two oracle feeds that charges whoever sells the weak coin into the pool, to fix the shadow truth labeler and the cost constants, and to treat any Unichain deployment as a cheap live testbed and an option on the chain's revival rather than a carry trade.

## LPs net 0.65 bp per dollar on a $400-a-month pie

Stable-pair LP on Unichain is **net positive at every fee tier measured**. In the main pool, LPs lose **0.35 bp per dollar of notional to adverse selection before fees** and keep about two-thirds of the 1 bp fee. The result holds when the reference changes: against Coinbase it is 0.361 bp of adverse selection and +0.639 bp net, and against OKX 1-second prices over the last 7 days it is 0.39–0.41 bp and +0.60 bp net ([OKX 1-second candles](https://www.okx.com/api/v5/market/history-candles?instId=USDC-USDT&bar=1s); [Coinbase candles](https://api.exchange.coinbase.com/products/USDT-USDC/candles?granularity=60)). The markout barely changes between 0 s and 1 h (0.356 → 0.351 → 0.332 bp). So the LP's loss is the instantaneous mispricing at execution, which latency arbitrage closes. It is not informed flow running ahead of a price move. The adverse-selection-to-fee ratio is about 35–40% in the main pool and about 90% on Slipstream, where LPs barely broke even. The sub-bp pools see only 0.01–0.05 bp of adverse selection, but that likely reflects what they attract: tiny bot legs arbitraged against the 1 bp pool, not order flow that would survive a change in where prices are set.

The measured pattern matches theory. Loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR) for a full-range AMM is σ²/8 of pool value ([Milionis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06046)). For USDC/USDT at a daily-sampled **σ ≈ 2.15 bp/day** ([Binance klines](https://data-api.binance.vision/api/v3/klines?symbol=USDCUSDT&interval=1h&limit=5)), concentration raises that to roughly 4.2% a year of in-range capital for a ±1 bp range. Arbitrage, however, pays most of it back as fees. With a fee γ, the probability that a block holds an arbitrage trade is P_trade = 1/(1 + √(2/Δt)·γ/σ) ([Milionis, Moallemi & Roughgarden](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14604)). At a 1 bp fee and 1 s blocks that comes to **0.5–0.7%**, so the LP's net arbitrage loss is about 0.02% a year of in-range capital. Fixed block spacing, which Unichain has, minimises LVR among block-time distributions ([Nezlobin & Tassy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05113)). Diffusive adverse selection therefore does not decide stable-LP profitability on Unichain. What decides it is fee share, flow, opportunity cost and depeg jumps.

A pool that wants better economics on Unichain needs more of that uninformed flow, which is a routing and depth problem, more than it needs a smarter tax on the bots.

Fee share inside the pool is extremely concentrated in tight ranges, and that concentration is the only niche that works. One EIP-7702-delegated EOA, `0xeb41232c…7e9e`, re-ranged about once a day through the official PositionManager, holding about $8.5k in 1–2-tick positions. It earned **51.9% of all pool fees** ($67.24 gross, $32.72 net of 5-minute markout), which is **9.6% gross and 4.7% net APR** ([Unichain RPC](https://mainnet.unichain.org)). By capital it ranks only sixth, with $6.5k or 4.1% of TVL in the position snapshot; by fee share it ranks first. Passive wide liquidity, which is most of the $160k, earns about 1.0% fee APR on TVL and about 0.7% after adverse selection. The arithmetic of scale is then simple (computed from the figures above). At 0.65 bp net, $10k needs $16–21k a day of captured flow to clear a 3.7–5% hurdle, which is about 35–50% of the pool. $20k needs $31–42k a day, which is nearly all of it. **$100k needs $156–211k a day, 3.6–4.9 times the pool's entire volume.** The capacity of the profitable niche is therefore about $10–20k, worth a few hundred dollars a year.

Unichain's 1 bp tier survives because no deeper, cheaper venue exists on the chain. That is why the profitable niche is depth at the market price rather than a fee cut. The case for sub-bp fees has support. The LP-economics note argues aggregators will route around a ≥1 bp pool in calm markets, and Campbell et al. find the profit-maximising fee sits near the competing venue's cost ([arXiv 2508.08152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08152)). On Unichain, though, the competing venue for an on-chain swap is the 1 bp pool itself. Halving the fee halves revenue per dollar on a market of about $43k a day that shows no evidence of elastic demand. Higher tiers keep more per dollar (0.17 → 0.38 → 0.65 → about 4.2 bp net across tiers). But the 5 bp pools see almost no flow: the v3 5 bp pool has $65.8k of TVL and traded $8.2k in 30 days.

## One-second blocks and $0.0007 swaps make LVR and gas irrelevant

Unichain produces blocks every **1.000023 s** (mean over the last 86,400 blocks). Each block is split into 200 ms Flashblocks, built by a Flashbots Rollup-Boost builder running inside a trusted execution environment (TEE). The TEE builder has been live since 1–2 May 2025 and Flashblocks since 14 August 2025 ([Unichain Flashblocks docs](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/unichain/technical-information/flashblocks); [Uniswap blog](https://blog.uniswap.org/flashblocks-are-live)). Within each Flashblock, transactions are ordered strictly by priority fee: this held in **all 82 of 82** segments that contained at least two user transactions. Across whole blocks it did not hold in 43 of 90 cases, so the effective rule is arrival time first, then fee, at 200 ms granularity ([Unichain RPC](https://mainnet.unichain.org)). There is no public mempool. `eth_sendBundle` accepts only a single transaction and provides revert protection, not atomic backruns ([Flashbots](https://collective.flashbots.net/t/bundles-are-coming-to-unichain/5072); [Unichain docs](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/unichain/technical-information/advanced-txn)). Third-party sandwiching is therefore structurally hard. Arbitrageurs compete on latency rather than tips: bots pay roughly 0.0005–0.00125 gwei, 5.1% of non-builder transactions failed in the sample, and revert rates were about 40% in mid-2025 ([arXiv 2506.01462](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01462v4)).

Gas is effectively free. The base fee has been pinned at a **0.0005 gwei floor** since 21 January 2026, the chain runs about 1% full against a 60M gas limit, and wallets pay about 0.00195 gwei. A plain v4 swap uses about 150,610 gas, or **$0.00074**, of which the L1 data fee is 0.8%. A mint costs about $0.0012, and all 379 LP transactions in the main pool over 30 days cost about $1.5 in total. No 6-hourly base-fee window since launch has exceeded 0.005 gwei ([Unichain RPC](https://mainnet.unichain.org); [growthepie](https://api.growthepie.com/v1/fees/table.json)). Two of the repo's cost assumptions are wrong in ways that roughly cancel. [`shadow/chain_cost_report.py:10-11,47-48`](../shadow/chain_cost_report.py#L10-L48) sets `PYTH_UPDATE_GAS = 90_000`, assumes "Unichain low" at 0.005 gwei and "stressed" at 0.05 gwei, and prices ETH at $3,500. Measured on-chain instead, a single-feed Pyth `updatePriceFeeds` costs **195,193 gas** standalone (about 174k incremental inside a swap), two feeds cost 221,830 gas, the typical gas price is 0.00195 gwei, and ETH is $2,499. In USD the errors offset to within about 3%. The overhead still matters for routing: a swap through the hook with an in-hook Pyth update costs about $0.0023, which is 0.225 bp of a $100 swap, and the median swap is about $130.

Moving inventory is cheap for USDC and opaque for USDT0. Unichain is CCTP domain 10. Fast Transfer out of Unichain takes about 8 s and costs **2 bp**; into Unichain it costs 1–1.4 bp. Standard transfers are free and take 15–19 minutes ([Circle CCTP domains](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/concepts/supported-chains-and-domains); [Circle Iris fee API](https://iris-api.circle.com/v2/burn/USDC/fees/10/0)). USDT0 moves over LayerZero. Its Unichain send path waits 1,600 block confirmations, about 27 minutes, and no primary source gives its fees ([Unichain RPC](https://mainnet.unichain.org)). The nearest operational event is **29 October 2026**, when Optimism takes over Unichain's sequencer, RPC and incident response under OP Enterprise. Chain ID, contracts and Uniswap deployments stay the same, but the announcement says nothing about whether Flashblocks, the TEE builder or bundles continue ([Uniswap blog](https://blog.uniswap.org/unichain-is-upgrading-to-op-enterprise)). The public RPC is already unfit for production. It caps `eth_getLogs` at 10k blocks, once returned 0 logs for a range that minutes earlier returned 3,248, and serves a `pending` block 8–20 days stale.

## Oracle delivery, USDT0 and depeg tails carry the real risk

**Pyth is the only provider found that publishes both legs, plus a dedicated USDT0/USD feed**, but on Unichain nobody pushes it. In the Unichain Pyth contract `0x2880…7B43`, USDC/USD and USDT/USD were last updated on **2 September 2025, 406 days ago**. USDT0/USD, DAI/USD and USDS/USD were never pushed and revert with `PriceFeedNotFound`. The update fee is 0 wei, and the price validity period is 60 s ([Unichain RPC](https://mainnet.unichain.org); [Pyth contract addresses](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/contract-addresses/evm)). Since 26 August 2026, Pyth's Hermes service has required an API key: a free trial, then paid plans. Unauthenticated requests returned HTTP 401, and Unichain's contract is listed as "Not upgraded" ([Pyth upgrade guide](https://docs.pyth.network/price-feeds/core/upgrade/preparing)). Chainlink on Unichain offers only USDC/USD, with a 24-hour heartbeat and 0.3% deviation threshold, so it cannot form a USDC/USDT ratio. It does provide the **L2 sequencer uptime feed** at `0x4956…F813` ([Chainlink directory](https://reference-data-directory.vercel.app/feeds-ethereum-mainnet-unichain-1.json)). No verified Unichain stablecoin feed addresses were found for RedStone, Chronicle, API3, Stork or eOracle.

Feed quality itself is good. The Pyth-derived ratio tracks OKX's USDC-USDT price to within 0.16 bp on average and 0.76 bp at p99, minute by minute. The repo's "feed noise ≈ 0.2 bp" is the right size for short-horizon jitter but the wrong yardstick for a breaker that compares the price with 1.0. Over 151 calm days on Sonic, the Pyth USDC/USDT ratio sat **|R−1| = 6.47 bp at the median, 12.87 bp at p99 and 15.73 bp at most** away from parity. Its 1-norm confidence was 11–24 bp at p50–p99, and it widened to 90.5 bp on 10 October 2025 ([Sonic RPC](https://rpc.soniclabs.com); [OKX](https://www.okx.com/api/v5/market/history-candles?instId=USDC-USDT&bar=1m)). The cause is USDT's 2026 discount: Coinbase's quarterly median USDT-USD was 4.5–7.4 bp below $1, and OKX's daily mean USDC-USDT rose from +0.8 bp to +11.0 bp in the week to 13 October ([Coinbase](https://api.exchange.coinbase.com/products/USDT-USD/candles)). A 50 bp trip therefore has about **3× headroom over the calm level, not the 143× (50/0.35) that [`shadow/stable_opportunity_report.py:23-26`](../shadow/stable_opportunity_report.py#L23-L26) gates on**, and the regime can shift 10 bp in a week.

Depeg history gives the breaker its calibration. Reserve-driven depegs crossed 50 bp **3.6–18 hours before their trough** and stayed beyond it for **14–110 hours**, re-crossing many times. Liquidity dislocations last minutes to about an hour, and most CEX spikes beyond 50 bp are single-minute prints.

On 10 October 2025 the Pyth ratio was beyond 50 bp for about an hour, in two excursions with a low of −65.1 bp. Its fastest onset was about 11 bp per minute, and between the excursions it fell back to 18–42 bp for 42 minutes ([Sonic RPC](https://rpc.soniclabs.com)). Binance-only crashes do not reach aggregated feeds or on-chain venues. When USDe printed $0.65 on Binance that day, Curve, Fluid and Uniswap deviated by 30 bp or less ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/usde-depeg-oracle-issues-ethena-founder)).

A concentrated stable LP is effectively short a put on each leg. When one coin drops to 1−J, arbitrageurs sell it into the range at about par until the range is exhausted. The LP ends fully in the weak coin, with a mark-to-market loss of about **J/2 against a 50/50 hold**: about 6% at SVB's $0.88 trough. If the coin re-pegs, that loss reverses, and panic flow becomes a windfall: USDT-USDC was among Uniswap's most lucrative pools in March 2023, with $2.4M in fees ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/123327/curve-finance-uniswap-trade-volumes-soar-usdc-depeg)). A fixed 50 bp guard fee only moves the conversion price by 50 bp against a 3–35% gap. Protection has to come from pricing at the oracle, one-sided halts or withdrawal.

Unichain adds its own layers of risk. **USDT0** is a LayerZero OFT backed by USDT locked on Ethereum. On all 14 pathways probed, it requires three of three DVNs to sign (LayerZero Labs, Canary and USDT0). Both the token and the OFT are upgradeable EIP-1967 proxies ([Unichain RPC](https://mainnet.unichain.org); [LayerZero DVN metadata](https://metadata.layerzero-api.com/v1/metadata/dvns); [USDT0 security](https://docs.usdt0.to/technical-documentation/security)). A bridge, DVN or upgrade failure could therefore create unbacked USDT0 without moving USDT/USD at all. For context, Kelp's rsETH OFT lost about $292M in April 2026 under a 1-of-1 DVN configuration ([ThreatCluster](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/usdt0-enhances-security-architecture-after-kelp-incident-6dd325a4)). Native USDC carries Circle's blacklist and pause roles. The legacy OP-bridged "USDT" at `0x588c…5518` is a third USDT that trades persistently 13–15 bp away from OKX. The sequencer uptime feed records **20 down episodes totalling 126.5 minutes** between February 2025 and September 2026, with a median of 4.4 minutes and a maximum of 27.0 minutes on 7 August 2026. Forced inclusion through L1 can take up to 12 hours, and upgrades have no exit window ([L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/scaling/projects/unichain)). Hook code is a tail risk in its own right. Bunni, the one stable hook that won share on Unichain, lost about $8.4M to a rounding bug in September 2025 and shut down the next month ([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/bunni-incident-repeated-small-withdrawals-compound-a-rounding-error-into-an-8.4m-drain); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/375813/bunni-dex-shuts-down)).

## No incentives, few rivals and a 4% hurdle sink the carry trade

Competition on Unichain is thin. Aave, Fluid, Balancer, PancakeSwap, Ekubo, Angstrom and Arrakis are not deployed there. LP-manager TVL is negligible: Gamma has $3.4k, Steer $10 and Arcadia $83 ([DefiLlama protocols](https://api.llama.fi/protocols)). The live dynamic-fee competitor to watch is Solo Labs' **Aegis** hook, with $1.71M of TVL, although no note could confirm that it runs a stable pool ([DefiLlama Aegis](https://defillama.com/protocol/aegis-markets)). The precedent is mixed. During April–July 2025, Bunni's hooked USDC/USDT0 pool won about 40% share and a **4.14% base yield against 1.66%** for the vanilla pool ([Gauntlet retro](https://www.gauntlet.xyz/resources/gauntlet-generated-33b-in-volume-for-unichain)), and then it was exploited. The bigger strategic risk sits off-chain. Uniswap Labs' **StablePair** hook launched on Ethereum on 10 September 2026. It quotes a fixed spread around a reference rate, charges no fee on trades that push the price further outside the band, and auctions corrective trades per block. It is not on Unichain, but if deployed there it would compete with PegGuard for the same router flow ([Uniswap blog](https://blog.uniswap.org/stablepair-hook-a-fee-that-moves-with-the-market)). Aggregators routed **$36.7M on Unichain over 30 days**, about 11% of DEX volume: KyberSwap $13.9M, 0x $13.3M and Uniswap's aggregator $6.8M ([DefiLlama aggregators](https://api.llama.fi/overview/aggregators/Unichain?excludeTotalDataChart=true&excludeTotalDataChartBreakdown=true)). A dynamic-fee hooked pool needs explicit quoter support from each of them.

Incentives are gone, and nothing suggests they will return. The Merkl endpoint for chain 130 reports `liveCampaigns: 0` ([Merkl](https://api.merkl.xyz/v4/chains/130)). Gauntlet distributed 4.23M UNI on Unichain, including **455,281 UNI to the main stable pool** between April and September 2025, worth about $2.8M at the programme's average $6.23 per UNI ([Merkl campaigns](https://api.merkl.xyz/v4/campaigns?chainId=130&items=100)). The proposal to extend the campaign infrastructure was cancelled on 10 March 2026, and a delegate called further Unichain incentives "much harder to justify" ([Uniswap governance](https://gov.uniswap.org/t/extension-of-uniswap-incentive-campaign-infrastructure-univ3-univ4-merkle-router-oracles/26048)). Protocol fees, by contrast, slightly favour a hook. Unichain is not covered by the v2/v3 fee expansion (Proposal 94, executed 7 March 2026) or by v4 fee activation Part 1 (Proposal 100, executed 27 July 2026). Proposal 100's fee families include only static-fee hookless, CCA and aggregator-hook pools, so dynamic-fee hooked pools fall outside them ([Proposal 94](https://vote.uniswapfoundation.org/proposals/94); [Proposal 100](https://vote.uniswapfoundation.org/proposals/100)). Unichain LPs keep 100% of fees today. If Unichain were ever added under the current rules, a static 1 bp pool would give up 0.25 bp (25% of LP fees), while a PegGuard pool would not.

The hurdle is about **3.7–4.0%**. The FOMC raised rates to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September 2026 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)). Tokenized T-bills pay 3.45–3.81%, sUSDS pays 3.80% (including $166k on Unichain itself), Aave USDC on Ethereum pays 3.68%, and curated Morpho USDC vaults on Base and Ethereum pay 4.2–5.2% ([DefiLlama yields](https://yields.llama.fi/pools); [Morpho API](https://api.morpho.org/graphql)). Unichain's own Morpho USDC vault pays only 1.12%, but capital moves freely over CCTP, so the hurdle is the cross-chain one. Measured against it, the verdict is clear. Passive stable LP on Unichain earns about 1% on TVL, a fifth of the hurdle. The active 1-tick niche earns 4.7% net on about $8.5k, roughly at the hurdle, before depeg, bridge and hook-risk premia. **All the stable fees on the chain add up to about $4.5k a year.** The ecosystem trend is also unfavourable. Unichain TVL has fallen from $902.9M to $26.8M, and its share of Uniswap v4 volume from 51.5% (May 2025) to 0.3%. Over the last 30 days, Robinhood Chain generated $51.2M of Uniswap v4 fees against $116k on Unichain ([DefiLlama TVL](https://api.llama.fi/v2/historicalChainTvl/Unichain); [DefiLlama Uniswap v4 fees](https://api.llama.fi/summary/fees/uniswap-v4?dataType=dailyFees)). Stablecoin market making on Unichain is worth doing only as a small, cheap testbed, and as an option on a revival such as new incentives, native interop after the OP Enterprise move, or a recovery in stablecoin float. It is not worth doing as a carry trade.

## PegGuard's GUARD breaker watches the wrong gap

Two notes, `oracles_depeg.md` and `lp_economics.md`, independently claimed that the GUARD breaker is flawed. I read [`src/PegGuardHook.sol`](../src/PegGuardHook.sol) and [`src/lib/SignalMath.sol`](../src/lib/SignalMath.sol) to check. What follows is a precise account of what the code does. **All four items are code-reading findings. No test has yet confirmed them in a depeg scenario. Foundry was not available in this environment, and no repository file other than this report was changed.**

**Finding 1 (code reading): the trip input is the pool's de-trended lag behind the oracle, not the oracle's distance from the peg.** In GUARD mode, the only trip condition is `absE2 > uint256(c.tripBps) * 100` ([PegGuardHook.sol:245-246](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L245-L246)). `devE2` comes from `SignalMath.deviationBps(_poolMidWad(id), fairWad, s.basisWad)` ([PegGuardHook.sol:221](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L221)), which computes `fair × basis / mid − 1` ([SignalMath.sol:36-41](../src/lib/SignalMath.sol#L36-L41)). `basisWad` is an exponential moving average (EMA) of `mid/fair`. It is updated after every swap that has a fresh oracle read, with time constant `tauSec` ([PegGuardHook.sol:270-277](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L270-L277); [SignalMath.sol:23-30](../src/lib/SignalMath.sol#L23-L30)). No line compares `fairWad` with 1.0. `PoolConfig` holds a single `pythFeedId` ([PegGuardHook.sol:45](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L45)), so the hook cannot form a USDC/USDT ratio. The README already lists a dual-feed ratio as an open item. As a result, the breaker trips when the pool sits more than 50 bp away from the oracle relative to its trailing basis. That happens when the oracle jumps while the pool is idle, which is the case the unit test exercises. It does not happen when arbitrage carries the depeg into the pool as it unfolds. On Unichain the USDT0 pools track OKX to within a 0.3–0.5 bp median, so a real depeg would rarely trip the breaker, and the EMA absorbs any persistent offset within roughly τ. That last point is an inference. There is also a practical issue: with no Pyth updates on Unichain, every swap would take the `STALE_OR_MISSING` fallback ([PegGuardHook.sol:318-319](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L318-L319)), so GUARD would do nothing until a keeper or the swapper supplies fresh prices.

**Finding 2 (code reading): once tripped, the guard fee falls on the side that does not drain LPs in an oracle-led depeg.** When the breaker is `TRIPPED`, `fee = c.guardFeePips` applies only when `!SignalMath.isCorrecting(devE2, zeroForOne)`. Correcting flow keeps the base fee set at line 232 ([PegGuardHook.sol:251-254](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L251-L254)). `isCorrecting` means the trade moves the pool's mid toward the de-trended oracle fair price ([SignalMath.sol:45-49](../src/lib/SignalMath.sol#L45-L49)), and the comment at line 252 calls that flow "re-pegging". This behaviour is intended and tested: [`test_tripped_deepeningPaysGuardFee_repeggingPaysBase`](../test/PegGuardHook.t.sol#L211-L220) lifts the oracle 1% while the pool stays put, and asserts that the swap moving the pool toward the oracle pays the base fee. The dispute is about the economics. Take token0 = USDC and token1 = USDT0, and let the oracle show USDT0 at 0.95. Fair is then about 1.0526 USDT0 per USDC against a pool mid of 1.00, so `devE2 > 0`. The "correcting" trade sells USDT0 into the pool for USDC. That is the arbitrage that drains the LPs' good asset, and it pays only the base fee. The guard fee lands on buying USDT0 from LPs at about par, a trade no arbitrageur would make. The logic is coherent for a dislocation local to the pool, where the oracle holds the peg and someone dumps into the pool. It is inverted for an oracle-led depeg, which is the event a depeg breaker exists for.

**Finding 3 (code reading): any oracle failure bypasses the breaker, including one that has already tripped.** `beforeSwap` returns the base fee at [PegGuardHook.sol:198-202](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L198-L202) whenever `_readOracle` fails. `_feeForOracle` runs only on success ([line 204](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L204)), and it contains both the trip check and the `TRIPPED` pricing. So during a failure the breaker can neither trip nor charge, even if it is already `TRIPPED`. `_readOracle` fails on a stale or missing price (lines 318-319), a non-positive price (line 304), or `CONF_SPIKE`, which fires when relative confidence exceeds 3× its EMA ([PegGuardHook.sol:309-311](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L309-L311)). The confidence EMA updates only in `afterSwap`, only for unflagged reads (the early return at line 310 precedes the update at lines 312-314), and with α = 0.1 per swap rather than per unit of time. A flagged gate therefore stays latched until confidence falls back. This follows the stated invariant to "never revert, never trade worse than a static-fee pool" ([PegGuardHook.sol:32-34](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L32-L34)). In GUARD mode, however, it switches the breaker off at the moment of stress. The oracles note's Python replication of this gate on Pyth data from Sonic flagged the USDT/USD leg on 15 of 172 observations on 10 October 2025. One flagged observation was 21:47:27, the first time the ratio crossed −51.6 bp ([Sonic RPC](https://rpc.soniclabs.com); [`scripts/oracles_depeg/analyze_breaker.py`](../research_notes/Unichain%20stablecoin%20pair%20market%20making/scripts/oracles_depeg/analyze_breaker.py)). That is a simulation of the gate logic, not a run of the contract. The repo's only test of this fallback, [`test_confSpike_fallsBackToBaseFee`](../test/PegGuardHook.t.sol#L117-L125), runs in HARVEST mode.

**Finding 4 (code reading, minor): ARMED does nothing, disarm checks nothing, and the basis drifts while tripped.** Pricing checks only for `TRIPPED` ([lines 246 and 251](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L246-L251)), so `ARMED` has no effect on fees. Disarm needs only a control-plane call held for `MIN_DISARM_DELAY` = 30 minutes, with no oracle check ([PegGuardHook.sol:354-368](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L354-L368)). `afterSwap` keeps updating `basisWad` while the breaker is tripped ([lines 270-277](../src/PegGuardHook.sol#L270-L277) contain no breaker condition). The deviation that decides which side pays therefore decays toward zero with τ, after which noise decides which side pays.

**The truth-labeling claim also holds as stated, within its stated scope.** `onchain_markout.md` says the repo's ±300 s centered-median truth inflates precision on sparse pools because the labeled swap's own post-trade price enters the basis. In [`shadow/truth.py`](../shadow/truth.py#L37-L62), `_compute_one` selects every ledger row within `±window_sec` (300 s by default, [line 11](../shadow/truth.py#L11)) and does not exclude the labeled row or its transaction (lines 39-47). It then takes the median of `post_mid/fair` as the basis (line 56) and labels the swap from its own `pre_mid` (lines 59-62). If the labeled swap is alone in its window, the basis becomes its own `post_mid/fair`, so `fair_local` equals its own post-trade mid. The deviation then always has the sign of the swap's own direction, and `truth_corr = 1` for every swap that moved the price. To check the effect of density, I ran the repo's own `process_delayed_truth` on synthetic swaps whose direction is random relative to a flat fair price, where an unbiased label should give about 50%. It returned **100% "correcting" when each ±300 s window held one swap, 83.5% at about three, 72% at about seven, 53.8% at about 31 and 50.5% at about 121**. The script is [`scripts/report_checks/truth_selfinclusion.py`](../research_notes/Unichain%20stablecoin%20pair%20market%20making/scripts/report_checks/truth_selfinclusion.py) (run as `python3 truth_selfinclusion.py <repo root>`), and the magnitudes depend on its toy assumptions. The note's Unichain evidence agrees. Replaying the τ = 450 s hook on the main pool for 7 days scored **98.9% precision and 437% "capture"** against repo-style truth, but only **50–61%** against labels that do not refer back to the swap itself (raw-OKX sign, toxic markout). With a leave-own-transaction-out basis, the realized ex-fee truth markout fell to 0.053 bp ([Unichain RPC](https://mainnet.unichain.org); [OKX](https://www.okx.com/api/v5/market/history-candles?instId=USDC-USDT&bar=1s)). The claim is about sparse pools, such as Unichain's main pool (median gap 80 s, with clustering). It is not a claim that the repo's Arbitrum baselines are wrong. The calm fixture averages one swap every ~14 s (629 swaps over 2h26m, [PARAMETERS.md:14](../research/PARAMETERS.md#L14)), about 40 per window, where the toy model shows only a few points of bias. I did not check whether the fixture generator excluded the swap's own transaction.

## Rebuild the breaker and size Unichain as an option

The repository needs one real redesign and several calibration fixes. PegGuard should not add calm-market directional alpha for stable pairs. Against honest truth labels, the τ = 450 s de-trended signal is close to random (50–61% precision). A raw-ratio premium would have raised **$50.85 in 30 days** in the main pool before any route-away, and bots that already cycle through sister pools would likely leave ([Unichain RPC](https://mainnet.unichain.org)).

Persistence costs at most 11–22 bp of extra move at the fastest observed onset, and it filters out every single-minute wick measured. A 25 bp disarm band would have kept the breaker tripped through the 42-minute lull on 10 October 2025, where a 50 bp band would have disarmed and then re-tripped. The "never worse than a static pool" invariant should be restated to apply only to flow that does not sell the weak coin. Keeper cost decides the delivery design (computed from measured gas). A three-feed push costs about $0.0012 at typical gas. Pushing every 60 s would cost about **$636 a year, roughly 40% of the main pool's entire annual LP fees**. Pushes triggered by price moves, plus a 10-minute heartbeat, cost about $64 a year. A breaker read can safely accept prices up to 60 s old when it is tightening, but should require 5–10 s freshness before loosening.

For the strategy, the right posture is to deploy code before capital. Start by pointing the shadow daemon at `0x77ea…59c9` with the corrected truth labeler and the two-feed oracle. Measure how GUARD would have behaved and whether routers would send flow to a hooked pool, using the repo's existing route-away tooling. Second, if live exposure is wanted, cap it at **$10–25k**. Concentrate it in 1–2-tick ranges centred on the market price (tick ~10 today) rather than the peg, and re-centre about daily as the USDC/USDT basis drifts. Gas of about $0.001 per LP operation is no constraint. Use the vanilla pool until the redesigned GUARD is tested, then a PegGuard pool at a symmetric **1 bp base fee** that competes on depth at the market price rather than on a fee cut. The incumbent has only ~$7k within ±1 tick, and break-even adverse selection of 0.35 bp leaves room to cut the fee later if route-away data justifies it. Third, choose the depeg policy explicitly. Until GUARD charges weak-coin sellers, the protection is an off-chain monitor of the Pyth ratio that withdraws liquidity on ARM. Withdrawal costs about $0.0007, but a 27-minute sequencer outage or a 12-hour forced-inclusion delay bounds how fast an exit can happen. Fourth, rebalance USDC over CCTP's free standard transfer and treat USDT0 as the harder inventory to move. Finally, scale only on a trigger: main-pool volume sustained near $170k a day, stablecoin float back toward $100M or more, native interop live after OP Enterprise, or a governance-funded campaign scoped to the PegGuard pool's own poolId. Merkl v4 campaigns bind to a single poolId and reward only in-range liquidity. The team can pitch such a campaign but should not underwrite it. A StablePair deployment on Unichain should prompt a fresh assessment.

## Conclusion

On Unichain, the scarce input for stable-pair market making is uninformed flow. Speed, liquidity and gas are not the constraint. The chain already removes most LVR, and the passive book sits anchored at a 1.0000 peg the market left weeks ago. The edge that remains goes to a few thousand dollars of 1-tick depth at the actual market price, and its capacity is too small to matter as a business. The economic case for a stable hook is therefore insurance, not harvesting. Turning a 3–12% depeg from "the LP sells the good coin at par" into "the LP sells at the oracle price" avoids about J/2, or 1.5–6% of position value, per event. That is more than a year of calm fees at any tier seen here, but the hook delivers it only if the breaker watches the oracle against the peg and charges the weak-coin seller. Today's code does neither.

The research also changes how the repo should judge itself. "No directional fuel for stables" holds on Unichain under honest truth labels. The 96–99% precision a centred ±300 s median reports on sparse pools comes from the labeled swap's own price impact entering its label, not from signal. Any future claim of stable-pair or low-density precision needs a leave-own-transaction-out truth. Finally, the redesigned GUARD works on any chain, and its value grows with TVL. Unichain is the cheap place to prove it works, and the larger prize for the same code is on Ethereum, Base or Arbitrum, where StablePair and sub-bp pools already compete for the volume.
